# Cell Classification — IMA205 Kaggle Challenge

A Kaggle challenge from the IMA205 Image Processing course at Telecom Paris. The task is to classify white blood cells from microscopy images into 13 types — BA, BL, BNE, EO, LY, MMY, MO, MY, PC, PLY, PMY, SNE, and VLY — distinguished by morphology: nucleus shape, cell size, and staining pattern.

The pipeline has two independent stages. First, a handcrafted segmentation step isolates the target cell from the background and surrounding red blood cells. Second, a residual autoencoder-classifier jointly learns to reconstruct and classify each segmented cell.

## Segmentation

The raw images contain multiple cells — typically one target surrounded by smaller red blood cells. Before classification, the target cell must be extracted and centered.

The segmentation pipeline runs in three steps:

**Score maps.** Two score maps are computed from color and spatial cues. The nucleus score highlights dark, saturated regions near the image center — characteristics of white blood cell nuclei. The cell score responds to purple and red-shifted regions outside the nucleus, which correspond to the cell cytoplasm.

**GrabCut.** The nucleus score is thresholded and processed with connected components to identify the best nucleus candidate. That candidate seeds GrabCut: the nucleus pixels are marked as definite foreground, pixels within an estimated radius of the nucleus as probable foreground, and the image border as definite background. GrabCut runs for two iterations and refines the cell boundary.

**Recentering.** The best connected component in the GrabCut mask is selected, cleaned with morphological operations, and the cell is translated so its centroid lands at the image center. The background is filled with the mean background color, with a soft Gaussian blend at the mask boundary.

## Model Architecture

The core model is an autoencoder-classifier. A shared encoder maps each segmented cell to a 512-dimensional latent vector. A decoder reconstructs the image from that vector, and a separate classifier head predicts the cell type.

  <figure>
    <img src="/assets/imgs/projects/ima205/reconstruction.webp" alt="Three columns: raw image, segmented input, reconstruction — for six cell types">
    <figcaption>For each class: the raw image (left), the segmented input fed to the model (center), and the decoder reconstruction (right).</figcaption>
  </figure>
  <figure>
    <img src="/assets/imgs/projects/ima205/pca_traversal.webp" alt="PCA latent traversal: six principal components each decoded at -3σ, -1σ, mean, +1σ, +3σ">
    <figcaption>Decoding along the six largest PCA directions in latent space. Each row is one component; columns sweep from −3σ to +3σ.</figcaption>
  </figure>
</div>

**Encoder.** Four residual downsampling stages reduce a 256×256 input to a 16×16 feature map with 512 channels. Global average pooling then flattens it to the 512-dim latent vector. Each stage uses a strided `ResidualBlock` with a 1×1 skip projection when channels change.

**Decoder.** A linear projection reshapes the latent vector to a 16×16 spatial seed, followed by four `ResidualUpsampleBlock` stages that progressively double the resolution. The output layer maps to three channels with a sigmoid, recovering the original image scale.

**Classifier.** A three-layer MLP with LayerNorm, ReLU activations, and two dropout layers (45% and 35%) reads from the latent vector and outputs class logits.

Training jointly minimizes the cross-entropy classification loss and the MSE reconstruction loss. The reconstruction objective acts as a regularizer — it forces the latent space to capture visual structure rather than overfitting to label-discriminative features.

  </figure>
</div>

Most morphologically distinct classes are classified reliably: LY (lymphocytes), SNE, EO, BA, BL, and MO all exceed 85% accuracy. The harder cases are classes with overlapping morphology — PMY and MMY (precursor/metamyelocyte), PLY (only 2 training examples), and VLY — which the model sometimes confuses with their nearest neighbors.

The PCA traversal shows that the first two components capture cell size and nucleus-to-cytoplasm ratio, and that the decoder can smoothly interpolate between meaningfully different cell morphologies.
